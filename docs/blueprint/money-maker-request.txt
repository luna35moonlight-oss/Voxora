## 2. How Alpha progresses from learning to trading

Your instruction to start slowly is exactly how I would structure the development process, with an important qualification: even months of profitable simulation cannot prove that future real trades will be safe.

Stage 1: Research and observation

Alpha monitors approved markets, evaluates sources, investigates strategies and explains potential risks. No orders are placed.

Stage 2: Paper trading

Trade using simulated funds and real or delayed market prices. Record performance, drawdowns, fees and execution assumptions.

Stage 3: Approved live trading

Once the user understands and accepts the risks, allow limited real-money trades through an authorised broker. Every proposed trade displays the instrument, quantity, estimated costs and potential downside before confirmation.

Stage 4: Controlled automation

Explore rules-based automated execution only where legally permitted, broker-supported, properly tested and subject to independent safeguards, hard limits, supervision and immediate shutdown.

I don't want Alpha deciding, based on a few profitable trades, that it's now ready to risk larger amounts. Trading permissions and budgets should never automatically expand because the AI feels more confident. Changes require deliberate user authorisation.

## 6. Alpha's opportunity ranking system

Not every opportunity deserves the user's time or money.

I want Alpha to rate opportunities using a transparent scoring model.

| Factor               | What Alpha investigates                              |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Legitimacy           | Is the business, client or platform genuine?         |
| Upfront cost         | How much money must the user spend?                  |
| Downside             | What can realistically be lost?                      |
| Skills match         | Does the user have the required abilities?           |
| Time commitment      | How much effort is needed?                           |
| Profit potential     | Expected revenue after realistic expenses            |
| Confidence           | How strong is the supporting evidence?               |
| Legal considerations | Licensing, taxation, platform policies and contracts |

Alpha should never present estimated income as guaranteed.

I also want a Scam Detection Gate. If an opportunity requires suspicious upfront fees, unrealistic investment returns or access to private banking credentials, Alpha must flag it before proceeding.

## 9. What we should build first

For Money Maker, I recommend this sequence:

First: Opportunity Radar, freelance research, financial tracking and a simulated trading environment.

Second: Digital product creation, portfolio building, invoice assistance and legitimate marketplace integrations.

Third: Broker connections with read-only portfolio access, followed by individually approved live orders if the regulatory and security requirements are met.

Fourth: Evaluate narrowly controlled trading automation only after substantial real-world testing, independent safety checks and any required authorisations.

Live autonomous investment management should not be an initial release feature.
